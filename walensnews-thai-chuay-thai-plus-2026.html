Policy Overview · 施策の概要
「ไทยช่วยไทยพลัส」とは何か ── 機能の仕組みを読む

The 60/40 Co-Payment Mechanism, Explained

On May 19, 2026, the Thai Cabinet approved Thai Chuay Thai Plus (ไทยช่วยไทย พลัส ฝ่าวิกฤตไปด้วยกัน — "Thais Help Thais Plus: Overcoming the Crisis Together"), PM Anutin Charnvirakul's largest domestic economic intervention to date. The program builds on the established Khon La Khrueng ("Half-Half") co-payment model but upgrades the subsidy ratio from the previous 50/50 split to a more aggressive 60/40, with the government covering the larger share. For every qualifying purchase at a registered small merchant, the government pays 60% and the consumer pays 40% — up to 200 baht of government contribution per day, 1,000 baht per month. A participant who maximizes the monthly subsidy contributes approximately 667 baht of their own money to unlock the full government top-up, implying roughly 6,667 baht in total stimulated consumption per person if fully utilized across four months.

Registration ran from May 25–29, 2026 via the "Pao Tang" (เป๋าตัง) mobile app, drawing 26.5 million applicants in five days — approximately 4 million short of the 30 million general registrant target, but sufficient for an active and meaningful program. State Welfare Card holders (13.18 million people) receive an automatic monthly top-up of 700 baht, requiring no re-registration. The spending window opened June 1, 2026 and runs through September 30.

Eligible merchants are restricted to registered small businesses — local eateries, general goods stores, community cooperative shops, and government-branded Thong Fah (ธงฟ้า) outlets — with annual revenues under 1.8 million baht. Excluded from participation: major modern-trade chains (Big C, Lotus's, Makro), all franchise convenience stores (7-Eleven, FamilyMart), and e-commerce platforms. Excluded product categories include alcohol, tobacco, lottery, fuel, and prepaid vouchers. All transactions must occur face-to-face via the "Thung Ngern" (ถุงเงิน) merchant QR system — no online or intermediary payments. One notable policy addition from mid-June: the co-payment is extended to food-delivery apps (Grab, LINE MAN, ShopeeFood, Robinhood) for food and beverage cost only — not delivery fees.

Key Intelligence Signals · 主要インテリジェンスシグナル
外資系企業がこの施策から読むべき3つのシグナル

Three Signals Every Executive Should Track Now

Opportunity · 機会
Grassroots consumer spending is about to accelerate — and the channel is traditional trade. Wet markets, small independent stores, and local restaurants will see the most direct uplift. Companies whose distribution networks reach these channels are positioned to capture a 4-month demand surge before Q4. The question is whether your inventory and trade-marketing plans are already calibrated for it.
Caution · 注意
The scheme creates a deliberate channel distortion that modern-trade operators must plan around. The exclusion of convenience chains and hypermarkets is not an accident — it is a policy choice to route subsidized demand away from large corporates toward small merchants. Companies whose core Thai accounts are large-format retail will see a relative drag while the program runs. Promotional calendars may need rebalancing toward the co-payment period.
Strategic Watch · 戦略的注視
Chinese firms are already positioned to intercept this demand — and the playbook is familiar. Independent economists estimate 15–20% of co-payment spending flows to Chinese-manufactured goods through legitimate Thai small shops. In automotive, Chinese brands hold 46.8% of the Thai market as of January 2026. The consumption window is an acceleration context for a competition that is already well underway. This is the part of the analysis that most executives are not yet reading closely enough.

The Fiscal Signal Behind the Stimulus

Thailand's 2026 GDP consensus is clustering at 1.6–2.1%, the lowest trajectory in years. Household debt stands at 86.8% of GDP — the highest in ASEAN — and private consumption growth is forecast at just 2.3–2.5% without intervention. The stimulus is not being launched from a position of strength. It is a defensive measure by PM Anutin's Bhumjaithai government, funded via an emergency borrowing decree of up to 400 billion baht, at a moment when public debt is approaching the 70% ceiling.
